State Highway 43 · Taranaki to King Country

The Forgotten World Highway

Stratford · 311m Elevation profile Taumarunui · 167m

148.6km between Stratford and Taumarunui, over four saddles and through a single-lane tunnel, on what was a bridle path before it was a road. The middle hundred kilometres has no fuel, no shop and almost no phone coverage: one pub at Whangamomona, a scatter of campgrounds, and a lot of country that emptied out when the railway closed. Every stop below is placed from OpenStreetMap, every detour is a real driving distance from OSRM, and every walk has been measured from its actual mapped route rather than from a brochure.

Getting home from the eastern end is the same either way. The northern loop out through Te Kuiti and the Awakino coast is 188km and about 2h35. Turning round and driving back over SH43 through Stratford is roughly 190km and about 2h38. The choice is scenery and daylight, not time.

Walk figures, and what they mean

Distance is measured node to node along the track's own mapped line. For the three tracks DOC manages that line is DOC's, and for the rest it is OpenStreetMap's. This matters: on the Moki Track, which is off the list for lambing, OpenStreetMap maps only the formed middle while DOC's line includes the 3km of farmland at each end, which is the difference between 11.8km and 18km. Where a track is walked out and back, the distance shown is the round trip.

Times marked DOC time are DOC's own published figures, fetched from their public API and baked into this page, so they work with no signal. They are the number on the sign at the trailhead. Everything else is Naismith's rule, 4km/h on the flat plus a minute per 10m of climb, which is a fit pace with no stops. Where both exist DOC is always the slower of the two, so treat Naismith as the optimistic end.

Climb is marked approximate on purpose. It is sampled against Open-Meteo's elevation model at 100m spacing, which is that model's own resolution. Sampling finer does not reveal more hill, it just accumulates the model's noise as imaginary ascent: on the Moki Track, 300m spacing gives 467m of climb and 25m spacing gives 1598m for the same ground. Distance and the altitude range are solid. Treat the climb figure as a way to rank tracks against each other, not as a number to plan on.

Tracks looked at and left off

The corridor genuinely does not have many formed walks. These were found in OpenStreetMap and deliberately excluded:

  • Rerekapa Track, which DOC grades Advanced at 3 to 4 hours, but it is 41.7km of gravel each way from the highway.
  • Mount Damper to Te Rerepahupahu Falls, DOC Advanced and 7h30 to 9 hours, and Waitaanga South Road to Te Rerepahupahu Falls, DOC Advanced and 4h30. Both are around 38.5km and 75 minutes of driving each way on top of the walk.
  • Sunshine Track at Taumarunui, tagged access=no in OpenStreetMap despite being signposted off the river walk.
  • Te Horo Stock Tunnel and Graylings Cattle Tunnel, both tagged access=no.
  • Whitecliffs Walkway, York Road Loop, Everett Park, PG Nops and Trimble, all on the New Plymouth side rather than this corridor.
  • Mangapurua Track, Matemateaonga Track and the Bridge to Nowhere, reachable by jet boat or a multi-day tramp, not from the road.

Freedom camping, in short

Freedom camping along SH43 falls under two councils. Stratford District covers the western half, roughly to the Tangarakau Gorge, and Ruapehu District covers the eastern half through Ohura to Taumarunui. Each has its own bylaws under the Self-contained Motor Vehicles Legislation Act, and a certified self-contained vehicle has far more options than one without. Camping markers here are a starting point, not a legal guarantee. Check current signage on the ground, and leave a site better than you found it.
